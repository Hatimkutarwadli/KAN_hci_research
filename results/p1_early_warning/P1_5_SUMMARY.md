# P1-5: extended early-warning evaluation (SYNTHETIC; EXTENSION, not part of the original six stages)

Code `extension_p15/{ew15,run_p15,report_p15}.py`; tests `tests/test_p15.py` (11 pass); results `results/p1_early_warning/{sust,cross,analysis}/`; run log `stdout_run.txt`, `config.json`. Tables: `analysis/primary_contrast.md`, `tables_sust.md`, `tables_cross.md`, `null_comparison.md`, `matched_exposure.md`, `threshold_uncertainty.md`; figures `analysis/fig_primary_contrast.png`, `fig_detection_by_lead.png`, `fig_horizon.png`, `fig_matched_auc.png`. **All numbers describe a simulator's latent threshold; nothing here is clinical or real-world early-warning validity.**

## Design (as approved)
- Saved predictions only (no retraining, no inference). 17 model families x 5 seeds (GBT 1): Stage 7 models (GBT, GRU, KAN, KAN-reg, M1, M1-static, KAN-GRU adaptive and fixed), Transformer, CNN-LSTM (P1-1), M1-MLP, B2-MLP, B2-MLP-reg, no-spline B2 (P1-3), single-task, budget-matched single-task and family GRUs (P1-2). Stage 9 v2 excluded.
- Stage 7 protocol unchanged, generalised to h in {7, 14, 21} with W = h (origins 27..117-h; sustained confirmation included; first-crossing 27..119-h). Threshold from calibration never-onset window maxima only; alarm iff score > tau; same cohort, episode, burden, precision definitions. Targets 10% and 20% false-alarm proportion. **Regression test first: all 164 saved Stage 7 rows (4,920 values) and every per-participant lead are reproduced exactly (h21).** (My first run of that test stopped on a wrong count bound I had written, 5000 vs the exact 4,920 = 164 x 30; all value comparisons had passed; the bound was corrected, not the protocol.)
- Primary contrast (fixed in advance): sustained onset, h21, 20% target, detection at lead >= 7 days (overall rate), depression and anxiety separately, vs GRU, paired stratified participant bootstrap (B = 1000, shared resamples, seed-pooled, thresholds fixed). Achievable-lead rate reported next to it.
- Nulls: iid U(0,1) of Stage 7; participant-trajectory shuffle null (200 permutations of complete score trajectories, shared by all models, temporal order and calendar alignment preserved, thresholds from the real calibration scores) - a null reference range, not a CI.
- Supplementary exposure-matched analysis: complete non-overlapping W-day blocks of the never-onset window (leftover days at the end unused: h21 3 blocks + 7 unused days, h14 5 + 7, h7 12 + 0); full-window onsets only.
- Threshold-uncertainty bootstrap (resample calibration never-onset participants, re-derive tau; seed 0, B = 300) kept separate from the participant bootstrap.
- First-crossing onset is a separate secondary analysis (`cross/`), only for models with first-crossing heads.

## Findings (test split; seed means; [ ] = bootstrap 95%)
1. **Primary contrast: no model differs from the GRU.** Detection >= 7 days at 20% FAP, h21 - depression (32 onsets, 139 never-onset): GRU 0.19 [0.07, 0.33]; Transformer 0.18, CNN-LSTM 0.19, M1 0.20, M1-MLP 0.17, KAN-GRU 0.19; differences -0.01 to +0.01, all intervals about +-0.07 and including 0. Anxiety (32, 132): GRU 0.30 [0.16, 0.46]; others 0.26-0.31; differences -0.04 to +0.01 (CNN-LSTM -0.04 [-0.10, 0.01], M1-MLP -0.04 [-0.11, 0.02]), all including 0. Other families: GBT dep +0.06 [-0.07, 0.21], anx -0.14 [-0.31, 0.01]; single-task/family GRUs -0.01 to -0.06, anxiety family -0.05 [-0.12, 0.00] (borderline). Lead 7 was achievable for 30 of 32 onsets, so achievable-lead rates are nearly identical (e.g. GRU dep 0.20, anx 0.32). Seed SD 0.02-0.08, comparable to the differences.
2. **Detection beats the iid null but, for long leads, not clearly the shuffle null.** The iid null detects 0.04-0.05 at lead >= 7, which makes every model look strongly above chance (the Stage 7 conclusion). Against the shuffle null (same models' score trajectories assigned to other participants; null mean about 0.14 [0.04-0.26]) depression lead >= 7 detection (0.17-0.22) lies inside the null range (observed above the null in 52-78% of shuffles); anxiety (0.24-0.31) is above it in 86-99% of shuffles. Detection at lead >= 1 day (any alarm in the window) exceeds the shuffle null in 95-100% of shuffles for both outcomes at all horizons: the signal is mostly short-lead. The shuffle null is much more demanding than the iid null because alarm-prone, persistent score trajectories also fire often by chance.
3. **Across lead times and horizons** (main models, 20% target, overall rates): detection >= 1 day 0.28-0.34, >= 3 days 0.25-0.32, >= 7 days 0.15-0.28, falling further at 14 and 21 days; median earliest lead among detected onsets about 10-15 days at h21 (3-10 detected participants per run, indicative only). Horizons differ little in detection at lead >= 1 (dep 0.32 / 0.28 / 0.29 and anx 0.28 / 0.31 / 0.34 for h7 / h14 / h21); they are not directly comparable because window length and never-onset exposure change.
4. **False alarms and burden.** Calibration thresholds give 0.198-0.200 calibration FAP; test FAP 0.16-0.22 against the 0.20 target. Threshold uncertainty alone (finite calibration sample) moves the test FAP over roughly 0.12-0.30 and lead >= 7 detection over roughly 0.12-0.38 for the GRU/M1. Alarm burden 3-4% of participant-days (never-onset), 0.3-0.4 episodes per 100 participant-days; participant precision 0.26-0.35 versus 0.06-0.08 for the iid null; day-level precision 0.13-0.17 and recall 0.11-0.19.
5. **Unequal exposure (supplementary).** The participant AUC is 0.57-0.62 for main models, but its chance level is well below 0.5 (shuffle null 0.25-0.34) because onset windows are at most W days and never-onset windows 70-84 days. Using exposure-matched blocks, discrimination of an onset window from a never-onset W-day block is AUC 0.77-0.86 (chance 0.5), block false-alarm rate 0.05-0.10 against full-window detection 0.28-0.41 (about 4-6 times higher). Even matched, this is modest warning performance.
6. **Multiplicity.** Of 768 sustained-onset detection contrasts vs the GRU (all models, horizons, targets, leads, outcomes) 69 have intervals excluding 0 (9%, expected order of 5% for independent comparisons; they are correlated); none is a primary contrast. Most are single-head / B2-type / family models being lower at short windows (h7, h14) or at lead 1-3, plus a few KAN-type models higher at lead 21. First-crossing (secondary): 79 of 624. These are descriptive and are not treated as differences between models.
7. **First-crossing onset (secondary, separate).** 41 evaluable onsets per outcome (test), 118-126 never-onset; detection >= 1 day 0.19-0.31, >= 7 days 0.14-0.24, participant AUC 0.54-0.62 for the main models; no model clearly differs from the GRU. It is not combined with the primary analysis.
8. **Relation to earlier steps.** P1-1: Transformer and CNN-LSTM do not detect better than the GRU. P1-2: the multitask average-precision advantage over single-task models does not turn into a reliable detection difference at these operating points (single-task models are nominally lower on anxiety, interval including 0). P1-4: the models carry no lag-specific information, consistent with detection being a short-lead, recency-weighted signal.

## Limitations
- 32 evaluable onsets per outcome (41 first-crossing); one participant moves a rate by 3.1 points; lead-time rates come from the same events and are nested; intervals are wide and several comparisons are made. 7-8 onsets have windows shorter than W and 5-6 fall after the last origin, so some leads are unachievable (see achievable counts).
- Bootstrap intervals are conditional on the trained seeds and thresholds; threshold uncertainty is shown separately and not combined; no retraining variance beyond 5 seeds; GBT is a single run.
- Thresholds come from 131-135 calibration never-onset participants whose data also served model selection; calibration was inspected in earlier stages; the test split was evaluated in earlier stages (no decision here used test outcomes: horizons, targets, estimators, primary contrast and nulls were fixed in the approved plan).
- Different horizons use different never-onset exposure (70/77/84 days); the exposure-matched analysis is supplementary and drops leftover days; full-window onsets are fewer (19-26).
- The shuffle null keeps each model's trajectories but breaks person-outcome links; it does not model between-person risk structure explicitly and 200 permutations give a resolution of about 0.5%. Exceedance fractions are not formal p-values. The iid null is weak.
- One synthetic dataset; sustained onset defined by the simulator's 3-day latent threshold; no clinical usefulness, real-world early-warning validity or causal claim is made.
