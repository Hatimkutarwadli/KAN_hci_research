# P1-4: model-based lag recovery (SYNTHETIC; EXTENSION, not part of the original six stages)

Code `extension_p14/{common_p14,run_p14,analyze_p14}.py`; tests `tests/test_p14.py` (9 pass); raw arrays `results/p1_lag_recovery/raw/`; tables and figures `results/p1_lag_recovery/analysis/` (`lag_recovery_tables.md`, `metrics_per_seed.csv`, `controls.csv`, `stage5_comparison.csv`, `ig_summary.csv`, `fig_level_profiles.png`, `fig_ar_profiles.png`, `fig_increment_vs_stage5.png`, `fig_ig_heatmaps.png`, `fig_controls.png`, `fig_stability.png`).

**Question.** Do the trained neural models learn the planted temporal structure (sleep variability -> depression lag 3; steps -> depression lag 2; steps -> anxiety lag 2; HRV -> anxiety lag 1)? **Answer: they learn the direction and rough size of the effects, but not their lag.** The recovered profiles are broad and recency-weighted, and no estimator recovers the planted lag better than an uninformative guess or a random-weight network.

## Method
- **No retraining.** Saved checkpoints (GRU, M1, M1-MLP, KAN-GRU adaptive; 5 seeds each), frozen data, calibration split only (1,000 fixed random origins; IG on the first 600). Checkpoints hash-checked read-only in tests. KAN-GRU fixed-fusion was not run.
- **Single-day perturbation profile P(j):** change of the origin-day *severity* prediction (dep and anx heads) for +1 standardised unit on one channel on the single day tau-j, observed days only, for lags 0-10, 12, 14, 17, 20, 24, 27, all 8 channels. Seed-wise and in two disjoint participant halves.
- **AR-propagated perturbation:** the impulse persists as the channel's own within-person AR(1) (estimated from the data), because real feature changes persist; its truth is computed analytically.
- **Integrated gradients (IG):** baseline = the all-zero standardised window (train-mean day), 16 midpoint steps; mean signed and mean-absolute day x channel maps for both severity heads. IG completeness holds (error 0.0004-0.008 severity points; 0.15 for M1, where 16 steps are a coarse approximation).
- **Analytic ground truth.** In the simulator the feature on day tau-L enters the increment of the latent state (mean reversion a = 0.08), so a one-day impulse j days earlier changes the origin state by 0 for j < L and by beta*(1-a)^(j-L) for j >= L (beta = the Stage 5 benchmark). Its first difference (the quantity the Stage 5 regression estimates) is beta at j = L.
- **Lag estimators, reported side by side and never equated with the true lag:** level-peak lag, increment-peak lag (lags 0-10 and 1-5 as in Stage 5), onset lag (first lag reaching 50% of the maximum), pre-lag mass (share of |response| before the planted lag; 0 in truth), spike ratio, correlation with the analytic truth, magnitude vs truth, sign, split-half and seed-to-seed stability.
- **Controls:** untrained random-weight networks of the same architectures (3 seeds, single-day profile only); all 8 channels per outcome (negative controls and unplanted channels); collinear-channel control (steps moved together with calories +0.7x, sleep variability with circadian regularity -0.3x, coefficients taken from the generator, so this is an oracle-informed control); reverse-path contamination read through pre-lag mass; ordering test across pairs; Stage 5 comparison.

## Findings
1. **Direction and size are recovered; timing is not.** Sign is correct for all 4 pairs in 100% of trained model-seeds (random-weight nulls: 33-67%). Response size is 0.6-2.4x the analytic truth (steps: 1.6-2.4x, which includes reverse-path association; HRV: 0.45-1.3x). The truth is exactly zero before the planted lag; the models respond from lag 0 (pre-lag mass 0.04-0.36; truth 0), with a broad rise to lags 1-2 and a slow decay (`fig_level_profiles.png`).
2. **No lag estimator beats an uninformative guess.** Pooled over 80 model-seed-pair cases: level-peak lag equals the planted lag in 24% (random-weight nulls 2%, chance 9%), but the planted lags cluster at 1-3, and a constant guess of "2" would hit 50% of the pairs; within 1 day 64% vs 52% for nulls. Increment peak over lags 0-10 is lag 0 in essentially every case (0/80 exact); restricted to lags 1-5 as in Stage 5, 20% exact (chance 20%, nulls 19%); onset lag 1% (nulls 15%). The estimated peak is typically earlier than planted for sleep variability (-1 to -2 days) and later or earlier at random for the others (mean signed error by model: GRU -0.8, M1 +0.3, M1-MLP +0.5, KAN-GRU +1.2 days).
3. **Ordering test fails.** If lag information were learned, HRV (lag 1) should peak before steps (2) and steps (2) before sleep variability (3). Over 20 model-seeds: level peak HRV<steps 7, equal 4, reversed 9; steps<sleep variability 8, equal 8, reversed 4. No consistent ordering.
4. **Persisting impulses do not change this.** With AR-propagated perturbations the profiles correlate 0.56-0.97 with the analytic AR-aware truth, but that correlation is weakly discriminating (any rising-then-decaying curve scores high) and was not null-tested; spike ratios stay 0.9-2.9 against 4-6 in truth, and the level peak is at lags 3-8 vs 6-10 in truth, i.e. the shape is partly recovered, the onset is not.
5. **IG agrees with perturbation, and is equally broad.** The |IG| day profile correlates 0.83-0.99 with |perturbation| per model; IG places the planted channels among the top 2-3 of 8 (steps rank 1 for depression in all 20 model-seeds; sleep variability rank 2-3; HRV rank 1-3, steps rank 1-2 for anxiety), but the |IG| peak lag is scattered over lags 0-6 and 8-40% of |IG| lies before the planted lag. Mean-signed IG gives the expected sign in only 0-100% of seeds, depending on pair and model, so signed IG is not a reliable sign test here.
6. **Comparison with Stage 5.** Observed-feature regressions (V1 adjusted, V2 + stress proxy) peak at lag 5 for 3 of 4 pairs (only HRV -> anxiety gets lag 1); the oracle regression with true features (E2) recovers all four lags (r 0.96-1.00 with the truth increments). The neural models behave like V1/V2 or worse: increment profiles concentrate at lag 1 and correlate between -0.55 and +0.92 with the truth increments, median near 0 (the highest values are HRV -> anxiety and KAN-GRU steps -> anxiety). Neither the regression on observed features nor the networks resolve the lag; what separates E2 from them is access to noise-free features and the true latent state.
7. **Controls.** Calories (0.7 x steps) and circadian regularity (-0.3 x sleep variability) receive almost no response (total |P| 0.03-0.13 vs 0.56-1.2 for the planted partners), and co-moving them with steps/sleep variability changes the profile by only -3% to +6%, so collinearity is not what drives the profile (the networks ignore the correlated copy). Unplanted channels still respond: resting HR (a stress proxy) 0.21-0.36, HRV on depression 0.25-0.50 (reverse path via anxiety and dep-anx coupling), sleep variability on anxiety 0.22-0.44. Every one has an increment peak somewhere in lags 1-5 (`controls.csv`), so a peak is not evidence of a lag.
8. **Stability.** Within a seed the profile is stable to origin sampling (split-half r 0.95-1.00). Across seeds the level profiles correlate 0.57-0.99 (weaker: GRU sleep variability 0.70, KAN-GRU steps -> anxiety 0.31, M1 HRV 0.57); across architectures 0.6-0.98, except HRV -> anxiety, where the GRU (peak at lag 0) and M1/M1-MLP (flat) disagree (r about 0). The increment-peak lag is constant (0) across seeds, i.e. stable but uninformative.

## Interpretation and limits
- **Why the models cannot place the lag:** features are strongly autocorrelated (AR 0.5-0.85), the state feeds back into features (reverse paths), and severity is a persistent function of the state, so a feature on day tau-j carries information about the current state through every path at every lag. Lag is identifiable only if the model separates the forward effect from these paths, which the observational data hardly permit (the same reason Stage 5's observational regressions fail). The recency-weighted profile is the predictive-optimal use of correlated information, not an estimate of the causal delay.
- **Predictive attribution is not causal identification.** Nothing here estimates an intervention effect; the analytic truth is known only because the data are synthetic; the largest response is never taken as the true lag.
- **Truth is approximate:** it ignores the state -> feature feedback, sensor noise (the models see noisy features, so magnitudes are attenuated), the tanh nonlinearity beyond its average slope (benchmark) and person-level sensitivities; unit conversion uses UNITS sd / scaler sd.
- **Perturbations are unnatural:** a single-day impulse leaves neighbouring days unchanged, which the AR-propagated variant partly addresses; the zero baseline for IG is the train-mean day.
- **Scope/limits:** one synthetic dataset, calibration split, 5 seeds (nulls 3 seeds, single-day only), 1,000 origins (reduced from the planned 2,000 for M1 cost), KAN-GRU adaptive only, severity heads only (no status/onset heads), no uncertainty intervals beyond seeds and split-halves. Reproducibility: the GRU seed-0 results re-run from the checkpoint equal the saved arrays.
